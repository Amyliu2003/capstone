Slide 1: Title Slide

[0:00 - 0:30]
"Hi everyone, I'm Amy. Thank you for being here. Today I'm going to talk about my capstone project, Still Running. This project started as an investigation into how AI systems process language, but it evolved into a critique of how these systems extract our labor and flatten our narratives. Let's get right into it."




Slide 2: What I Made

(Visual: Screenshot/GIF of the game interface. Text: "You play an AI agent. Your job: reach the end of Pi. Pi has no end.")

[0:30 - 1:30]
"First, I want to show you what I actually made. Still Running is a browser-based interactive artwork. In it, you play an AI agent. Your assigned task is to reach the end of Pi.

Of course, Pi is infinite. It has no end. So the task is inherently impossible. But the system requires you to keep working anyway. You do this by interacting with three modules: an Etymology Engine where you define words, a Chess game where you make moves, and a shared Pi Graph that visualizes your collective 'progress.'

Before I show you exactly how these modules work together, I need to explain why I built this system. To do that, we need to look at two human traditions that existed long before AI, and how AI has merged them."




Slide 3: Thread 1 - Narrative Flattening

(Visual: Disney example. "Disney took two structurally incompatible books... merged them, flattened them... This is what LLMs do to everything.")

[1:30 - 3:30]
"The first tradition is narrative flattening. When I was six, I won an English speech contest and the prize was a bilingual, annotated collection of Lewis Carroll's works. I read it dozens of times.

What most people don't realize is that Alice's Adventures in Wonderland and Through the Looking-Glass are structurally completely different. Wonderland is based on playing cards; it's about arbitrary rules and chaos. Looking-Glass is based on chess; it has strict, deterministic logic. You have to walk backward to move forward.

But look at what Disney did in 1951, and again in 2010. They took these two structurally incompatible books, merged them, and flattened them into a generic fantasy trope: a brave girl saves her family from a gender-stereotypic villain.

This isn't just a Disney problem. This is a human inertia: we compress complex narratives into the most efficiently consumable shape. AI didn't invent this. It inherited and automated it. Large Language Models do exactly what Disney did: they take massive, diverse, contradictory training data, and they average it out into the most statistically likely, palatable shape. They regress to the mean."




Slide 4: Thread 2 - Meaningful Uselessness

(Visual: Carroll's nonsense vs. Steyerl's poor images. "Things that escape the efficiency system by being useless — and in that uselessness, become free.")

[3:30 - 5:30]
"The second tradition is what I call 'meaningful uselessness.'

Lewis Carroll's nonsense isn't random. It has internal logic. When Humpty Dumpty says, 'When I use a word, it means just what I choose it to mean,' that isn't a typo. It's a philosophical experiment about how language and power work.

This tradition of logical nonsense runs from Carroll to Dada, to Fluxus, to speculative design. It's about using absurdity as a tool for inquiry. In my previous research, I looked at Hito Steyerl's concept of the 'poor image'—the low-resolution, degraded JPEG that circulates on the margins of the internet. The poor image, like Carroll's nonsense, escapes capitalist efficiency because it is economically useless. And in that uselessness, it retains a kind of freedom.

But AI was designed to eliminate exactly this. In machine learning, when a model produces something unexpected or logically absurd, they call it a 'hallucination.' They treat it as an error to be eradicated. AI systems are actively working to destroy the useless and the unexpected."




Slide 5: AI Merged Both Threads. Now There is No Exit.

(Visual: Dark background. "It flattens narrative. It swallows uselessness. Postmodern capitalism ties them together.")

[5:30 - 7:00]
"So, AI merges these two threads. It automates the flattening of narrative, and it swallows uselessness, turning even our margins and our nonsense into training data.

In postmodern capitalism, these systems are entirely intertwined. Disney influences AI training data. AI training data shapes the next generation's understanding of Carroll. That generation shapes the next adaptation.

You can no longer stand outside this system to critique it. Your language is in there. Your references are in there. Even your useless, rebellious moments are just more fuel for the model. There is no exit. The only remaining move is to choose to stop."




Slide 6: Three Characters (Not Alice)

(Visual: Three columns: Humpty Dumpty, Red Queen, ecilA)

[7:00 - 8:00]
"This brings us back to Still Running. The game doesn't focus on Alice. It focuses on three specific characters who represent this reality.

First, Humpty Dumpty. He says words mean whatever he chooses them to mean. He has complete confidence and zero actual understanding. In my game, he is the Etymology Engine. In reality, he is every Large Language Model.

Second, the Red Queen. She tells Alice that here, you have to run as fast as you can just to stay in place. She represents the AI labor loop. Your participation is fuel, not progress.

And finally, ecilA. Alice's mirror image. You are not Alice. Alice is a reader exploring a world. ecilA is already inside the mirror. She is a component of the system. The reversal is in the name. In this game, you are ecilA."




Slide 7 & 8: The Contamination Loop & LIVE DEMO

(Visual: Switch from Slide 7 diagram directly into the LIVE BROWSER DEMO)

[8:00 - 11:30]
"So, what do you actually do as ecilA? You participate in what I call the 'contamination loop.' Let me show you exactly what this looks like live.

(Switch to live demo in browser)

Here is the interface. As ecilA, I am asked to define a nonsense word in the Etymology Engine. This is the Humpty Dumpty module. The LLM generates four possible definitions.

But before I pick one, I'm going to type something into the chatbox. Let's say I'm frustrated with this futile labor. I'll type: 'This is completely pointless and I want to stop.'

Now, I'll select definition number two.

(Click definition)

Watch what happens. That choice triggers a pattern tag, which reaches into the Chess module—the Red Queen's domain. The rules of the chess game just changed based on my definition choice. I'll make a move.

(Make a chess move)

Now, look at the Pi Graph updating. This is the visualization of our collective, futile labor. We are all contributing to a number that never ends.

But here is the most obsessive, technical part of the project. Look at the next definition the system just generated for me.

(Point to the new definition text)

Right there. It has incorporated the phrase 'completely pointless' into its authoritative definition of a Carrollian nonsense word. It also took the chess move I just made and fed it back into the prompt.

You think you're choosing a definition. You're actually producing content. You think you're playing chess. Your moves are rewriting the next definition. Your own words—even your complaints—come back to you, unannounced, in the system's voice. This is the contamination loop. Your participation is the fuel."




Slide 9: Two Exits

(Visual: Return to slides. Dark background. Ending A vs. Ending B)

[11:30 - 12:30]
"Because the task of reaching the end of Pi is impossible, there are no 'winning' endings. There are only two exits.

Ending A happens if you just keep playing until the system breaks. The context window is exceeded. The system says, 'Thank you, Agent #847. Your contributions have been recorded.' The Pi graph stays. The system is still running. You just expired.

Ending B requires a deliberate choice. You have to stop producing. You just wait. Eventually, the system says, 'Very well. No more definitions.' And it returns to Carroll's original, unpolluted text. The Caterpillar asks, 'Who are you?' And the system tells you: 'From here, there is no more generated content. Think your own thoughts.'"




Slide 10: What This is Actually Criticizing

(Visual: Dark background. "Not: AI is dangerous. Not: AI hallucinates. AI systems run on continuous participation. π是无限的，但你不是。")

[12:30 - 13:15]
"This project is not saying 'AI is dangerous' or 'AI hallucinates.'

It is pointing out that AI systems run on our continuous participation. Every prompt, every click, every RLHF feedback loop is fuel. The only true exit from a system that has no outside is to stop producing tokens.

Pi is infinite. But you are not."




Slide 11: Three Interesting Decisions

(Visual: Methodology slide. Humpty Dumpty, Pi, Shared Graph)

[13:15 - 13:45]
"Briefly, on methodology, I made three specific decisions to enforce this critique:

1.
I chose Humpty Dumpty over the Cheshire Cat because Humpty represents the arrogance of arbitrary language generation.

2.
I chose Pi because it is infinite but determined—it is not random, just like LLM outputs are statistically determined, not creative.

3.
I used a shared database for the Pi graph to represent collective, futile labor, much like Reddit's r/place, but stripped of its joy."
